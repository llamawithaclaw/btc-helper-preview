Assumptions this planner uses
  • Assumption A1: the safety floor has two parts, and both must be met. (1) Cash: emergency cash of at least one month of essentials plus minimum debt payments. (2) Monthly room: take-home income minus essentials, minimum debt payments and flexible spending is $0 or more, so you are not spending more than you take home. Savings alone do not meet the floor while you spend more than you take home. The one-month figure is an editorial choice, not an evidence-derived optimum. Related research: Emergency Buffers, Borrowed Resilience, and Household Hardship (working paper, May 2026). That paper describes how often adults with and without emergency savings report hardship; it does not set this number. Our research uses two survey measures, whether an adult could cover a $400 expense with cash or its equivalent, and whether they have three months of emergency savings; neither tests a one-month floor.
  • Assumption A2: you choose the buffer target; 3 months is the default.
  • Definition A3: earmarked money is not emergency cash.
  • Borrowing capacity is not counted as buffer. This is our choice, not a finding: credit can bridge timing gaps, but its availability, cost and repayment are not guaranteed when a shock arrives. Our choice is stricter than the survey measure our research uses, which counts a credit card paid off at the next statement as cash or its equivalent (Mapping Household Financial Resilience).

Where does Bitcoin fit?

Nowhere, unless you want it to. This planner treats any Bitcoin plan as optional investing. It only shows money for optional investing, in any asset, when your safety floor is met, your buffer target is met, and money is left each month (monthly room above $0). A covered floor alone is not enough. A market read, including BTC Helper's, never overrides that.

Supporting research

  • Emergency Buffers, Borrowed Resilience, and Household Hardship Evidence: how often adults with and without emergency savings report hardship. An association in survey data, not proof that savings prevent hardship; context for A1, it does not set the number.
  • Mapping Household Financial Resilience Background: how the survey measures emergency cash, and why borrowing is treated separately here.
