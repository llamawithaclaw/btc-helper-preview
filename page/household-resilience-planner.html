    Evidence and explanation

    Why this matters

    The planner connects household inputs to the public research base so the output is traceable instead of unexplained.

    Research links

    Emergency buffers / SHED Borrowed Resilience (national credit trends, background) Quiet Tax Medical Fragility / MEPS Confidence Ledger Capstone synthesis

    Why these measures?

    The score is a transparent planning heuristic — not a credit score, financial plan, or Bitcoin return forecast.

    Show methodology

    The score rewards household conditions that make long-horizon accumulation more durable: positive margin, emergency liquidity, manageable debt pressure, survivable essential costs, and accumulation that does not consume all available slack.

    Cash-flow marginMonthly slack leaves room to meet surprises without borrowing or selling assets.
    Emergency bufferLiquid savings are treated as a base layer. In US survey data, adults without emergency savings report bill-payment problems, outside help and medical debt far more often than those with savings. That is an association, not proof that savings prevent hardship.
    Debt pressureRequired debt payments reduce flexibility, especially when paired with thin buffers.
    Essential costsHousing, food, utilities, insurance, and irregular essentials can crowd out long-horizon planning.
    Medical exposureMedical and insurance costs are recurring costs of staying protected. In public survey data, out-of-pocket medical costs take a larger share of income in lower-income groups.
    Accumulation durabilityThe planner favors accumulation that can survive stress without becoming forced selling, high-cost debt, or interrupted savings.
