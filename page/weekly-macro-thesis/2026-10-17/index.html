This week in one sentence

This week’s macro debate centers on how government interest payments and shifting capital routes affect financial conditions. Neither a rising dollar nor a larger interest bill establishes Bitcoin’s direction on its own; funding conditions and actual demand remain the useful tests.

What changed since last week

  • Higher interest rates can discourage private borrowing while increasing income paid to government bondholders. The balance depends partly on whether recipients spend, save, or reinvest that income.
  • Support for asset prices and persistent consumer inflation are different outcomes. A growing government interest bill does not prove that monetary restraint has stopped working.
  • Bitcoin and the dollar could strengthen together if changing capital routes benefit both. This is a conditional explanation to test, not an established trading relationship.
  • Bond purchases should be judged by their financing and balance-sheet effects; debt-management operations do not automatically imply fresh monetary stimulus.
  • Plans to expand energy production are not yet usable power. Delivery timelines and costs remain constraints on the real economy.

BTC Helper implications

  • Do not treat dollar strength alone as a reason to dismiss Bitcoin demand; check whether funding conditions are improving or tightening alongside it.
  • A fiscal-support narrative is more compelling when accompanied by sustained spot demand and orderly credit conditions.
  • The macro discussion provides context, not a change to BTC Helper’s daily signal.

Counter-thesis

  • Interest income may be saved rather than spent, while higher financing costs continue to restrain activity.
  • Dollar strength could reflect a scramble for cash rather than supportive capital inflows, leaving Bitcoin exposed to selling pressure.
  • Changes in financial infrastructure may take time and need not generate additional Bitcoin demand.

What would change it

  • Sustained Bitcoin demand alongside a firmer dollar and calmer funding markets would strengthen the capital-routing hypothesis.
  • Worsening funding conditions and weak spot demand would favor the liquidity-stress explanation.
  • Evidence of restrained spending despite rising government interest payments would weaken the inflationary fiscal-support argument.

Watch items

  • Dollar and Bitcoin direction alongside credit conditions and spot demand.
  • Short-term funding strains and the availability of collateral.
  • Whether government interest income supports spending or remains in financial assets.
  • How bond purchases are financed and whether they expand usable liquidity.
  • Energy-project delivery and power costs rather than capacity announcements alone.
