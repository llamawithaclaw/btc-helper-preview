Research · What the evidence can show

BTC Helper Evidence Research note v1, 2026-10-06

What the evidence can and cannot say about this model

“Is the model any good?” is really four separate questions. They have different answers, and a yes to one is not a yes to the others.

QuestionCurrent answerWhat it rests on
1. Does the live system run the reviewed rules correctly?Yes.For the reviewed releases in use from 2026-10-04 to 2026-10-06, the published reads came from the reviewed code and passed the live-data checks. This confirms operation, not investment results.
2. Can the frozen rules be re-run to give the same decision?Yes, in the supported study.The decision rules are identical, byte for byte, across the three releases studied. Re-running them on the inputs retained for two recent runs gave the recorded decisions each time.
3. Do we hold the inputs as they were known on past decision days?No.In both places where past evidence is kept, none of the 11 inputs the rules use has a past record that is proven to have been available before the daily decision time. Past prices are held as values recorded at publication time, which is after the decision time. Two recent retained runs (2026-10-04 and 2026-10-05) can be replayed, but are not certified against the decision time.
4. Does following the read lead to better outcomes?Cannot be assessed from the evidence held.Without question 3, a historical test cannot answer this. Study V1 therefore concluded: historical efficacy is not assessable from the retained evidence.

What this result does not mean

  • It does not mean the model was validated or shown to work.
  • It does not mean the model failed or is unreliable. No test of its results was possible, so none failed.
  • It does not mean the current rules are the best rules, or that changing them would be better or worse.
  • It means the evidence available today cannot answer the efficacy question either way.

What could change this

Evidence collected going forward, recording each day's inputs as they were known at decision time, could support a later study. That collection is designed but not switched on; it needs a separate decision. The study design requires any confirmatory test to be registered in advance, so its result cannot be tuned after the fact. No such test is registered yet.

Details for the technically curious

How the study worked
  • The decision rules were run in isolation from an exact code version, with no live data and no network, and every run recorded hashes of the code, the inputs and the output.
  • The rules use 11 inputs. One of them (True Market Mean price) is used by the rules but not listed in the model's register of inputs; the study counts it.
  • Five constructed historical test cases had no recorded values; the rules made no read for any of them, as expected. They test how missing data is handled, not results.
  • Two published reads from September 2026 were checked against the public record. They are consistent with it, but they are reconstructions assembled after publication, not decision-time records.
Provenance and verification

Research note v1, 2026-10-06. Summarises study V1, which was carried out in two separate internal reviews, with AI assistance, and cross-checked. It has not been externally peer reviewed. The study record is private; this page states its conclusions without publishing input values.
