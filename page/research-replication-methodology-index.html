Public Research Replication and Methodology Index

Purpose

This page is the trust scaffold for the public research library. It explains what each public document is trying to measure, what data posture it uses, what is already reasonably solid, and what still needs stronger replication or microdata validation.

The goal is to keep the public site readable while preserving clear boundaries between published syntheses, working papers, measurement notes, prototypes, and supporting material.

Status labels

  • Published: stable public synthesis or product explanation.
  • Working paper: substantive research draft that may still be revised.
  • Measurement note: narrow evidence, replication, or methods note.
  • Prototype: experimental tool or research-to-action surface.
  • Supporting material: deeper background behind a better starting point.
  • Superseded: preserved for context, but no longer the preferred entry.

Current publication boundary

The public site can include working drafts because the site is ours and can preserve clear boundaries. The rule is simple:

A working draft can be public if the page makes the status visible before the reader reaches the headline findings.

That means a working draft should have:

  • a status label near the top;
  • a short “what is solid / what is not final” box;
  • plain-English caveats before technical caveats;
  • no headline claims from exploratory diagnostic tables;
  • links to methodology notes when available.

Near-term roadmap

1. Curate the public library

Keep the public surface organized by status and purpose. Do not let the research page become a file dump.

2. Harden replication notes

Build methodology notes for the highest-value public artifacts first:

  1. SHED emergency-buffer work — archive scaffold now includes public README, variable map, and checksum manifest; next upgrade is clean-room rebuild and frozen release.
  2. Paper IX / Fragility Premium — revised CEX appendix is public; next upgrade is repeated-cross-section CEX/MEPS and outcome validation, not more aggregate CPI polishing.
  3. MEPS medical financial fragility working draft — the latest revision adds selected survey-design confidence intervals, family-construction checks, and PRPL linkage sensitivity.
  4. Capstone synthesis source map.
  5. Monetary-stress paper series source index.

3. Polish the capstone synthesis

The capstone is the best user-facing entry point once the library is curated. It should explain how the papers fit together rather than adding another chartbook.

4. Prioritize SHED microdata as the flagship empirical backbone

The SHED lane is now BTC Helper Research's strongest household-level empirical lane. Read the SHED sequence in layers: the published-indicator note motivates the buffer problem, the replication/harmonization notes document the data path, and the hardship-profile working draft develops the main measurement contribution. The likely consolidated flagship paper is: Emergency Buffers, Borrowed Resilience, and Household Hardship: Evidence from SHED 2015–2025.

5. Return to MEPS for the next validation pass

The MEPS working draft now includes a first round of public checks on uncertainty, family construction, and premium linkage. The next MEPS revision should broaden design-based inference to means, thresholds, and models; validate family construction against MEPS guidance; check PRPL premium annualization and documentation; and extend beyond 2022.

6. Maintain a living sound-money research roadmap

BTC Helper should always have at least one active public-data research lane, even at a slower cadence than the initial sprint. Prioritize depth, replication, and clear data paths over paper count.

7. Explore the BTC Helper Budgeting App / Household Resilience Planner

A future BTC Helper product lane should explore an open-access, privacy-forward budgeting tool that helps users translate the research into practical household resilience decisions. It should be separate from any private/local budgeting workflow, though it can borrow general design lessons.

Public trust rule

When in doubt, underclaim. A reader should always be able to tell whether a document is a polished public note, a working draft, a methodology note, or an exploratory diagnostic.
